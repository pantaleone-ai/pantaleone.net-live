# Leaked System Prompts: A Look Inside AI

**System prompts** are the underlying instructions that define an AI's behavior, personality, and limitations. While typically kept confidential, a number of these prompts have been made public through repositories like [leaked-system-prompts](https://github.com/pantaleone-ai/leaked-system-prompts). Analyzing these leaks provides valuable insight into how different AI models are built and controlled.

## A Look at the Repository
The "leaked-system-prompts" repository contains over 60 files detailing prompts from major AI developers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. These files offer a timeline of how AI instructions have evolved.

# Key Findings from System Prompts

## 1. Common Design Patterns
Across different models, several common themes emerge in system prompts:

- **Identity**: Prompts usually define the AI's name and creator, such as "Assistant is a large language model trained by OpenAI" [[source](https://github.com/pantaleone-ai/leaked-system-prompts/blob/main/openai-chatgpt_20221201.md)].
- **Knowledge Cutoff**: Most prompts specify a date beyond which the AI has no knowledge.
- **Response Formatting**: Instructions often guide the AI on how to format its answers, for example, using Markdown for code.
- **Ethical Guidelines**: All major models include rules to avoid generating harmful, biased, or illegal content.

These similarities suggest an emerging industry standard for designing safe and functional conversational AI.

![System prompts can be model-specific and help drive conformance to guidelines.](https://pantaleone-net.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/blog-images/aisystem-prompts2.jpg "System prompts are useful")

## 2. Unique Characteristics of Different Models
The differences between prompts are often the most revealing:

- **xAI’s Grok 3**: This model's prompt explicitly names public figures it considers sources of misinformation, a direct approach not seen in other models [[source](https://medium.com/@Michael_Ram/leaked-system-prompts-from-xais-grok-3-df226e9f6f19)].
- **OpenAI’s ChatGPT4o**: Its prompt includes detailed rules for image generation with DALL-E, such as avoiding the styles of modern artists [[source](https://github.com/jujumilk3/leaked-system-prompts/blob/main/openai-chatgpt4o_20240520.md)].
- **Anthropic’s Claude**: Its prompt contains nuanced instructions on handling sensitive social topics and avoiding stereotypes [[source](https://github.com/jujumilk3/leaked-system-prompts/blob/main/anthropic-claude-opus_20240306.md)].

These differences highlight the varying priorities of each company, whether it's directness, creative control, or social harmony.

## 3. The Evolution of Prompts
System prompts are not static; they change over time to reflect new features and refined controls. For example, knowledge cutoffs are updated, and new capabilities like image generation are added. This evolution shows how developers adapt to new technologies and user feedback.

## 4. The Debate on Security and Transparency
The leaking of system prompts has sparked a debate in the AI community.
- **Security Concerns**: Exposed prompts could potentially be exploited by malicious actors to bypass an AI's safety features [[source](https://www.sydelabs.ai/blog/why-system-prompt-leaks-are-bad)].
- **Trust and Reliability**: If an AI's core instructions can be easily manipulated, it may erode user trust [[source](https://learnprompting.org/docs/prompt_hacking/leaking)].
- **The Case for Transparency**: In contrast, some companies like Anthropic have begun publishing their prompts to promote openness and trust [[source](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/26/anthropic-publishes-the-system-prompt-that-makes-claude-tick/)].

This tension between security through secrecy and trust through transparency is a central issue in AI development today.

# Conclusion
Leaked system prompts provide a valuable window into the inner workings of AI models. They reveal standardized design practices, unique corporate philosophies, and the ongoing evolution of AI safety and capabilities. For developers and users, understanding these prompts is key to grasping the technology's potential and its limitations.
